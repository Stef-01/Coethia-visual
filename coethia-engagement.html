Working With Coethia

The Loop in Detail, the Ad Machinery With Its Real Constraints, and Twelve Weeks From Brief to Reported Outcome

The companion piece argued that health communication should sort people by belief instead of by label, and ended by naming the system that would close that loop. This one is the operational answer to the obvious next question: what would actually happen if you hired us?

Every screen below is the real console, drawn as you would meet it — including the constraints that make public-health advertising harder than commercial advertising, and the one that makes it cheaper.

What You Are Actually Buying

Not impressions. Not a campaign. A loop — a listening intake that tells you what your population is actually asking this month, a segmentation that sorts them by the barrier they face rather than the label they carry, a message built per barrier from one evidence base, a buy that puts it in front of them at the moment of intent, and a measurement layer that reports comprehension and care-seeking instead of reach.

The machinery is ordinary. It is the same auction, the same audience tools, the same creative testing discipline that sells everything else on the internet. What is unusual is pointing it at whether someone understood, and then publishing the result when it did not work.

The engagement ends with your team able to run it without us. That is the design goal, and it is why the tiers ladder upward into training rather than dependency.

Start With the Listening

Most engagements begin with a two-week listening intake: what your population is searching, which beliefs are load-bearing, and where the access gaps sit. It is the cheapest way to find out whether the rest is worth buying.

What Is Real and What Is Illustrative

The client is composite. “Marion County Public Health” is invented to carry the walkthrough, and every metric shown inside the console — audience sizes, costs per action, comprehension lifts, timelines — is synthetic and generated in this file. They are structurally plausible placeholders, not results.

The platform mechanics are real and documented. Google Ad Grants terms, custom-segment inputs, the health-condition targeting restrictions, Ad Rank and Quality Score arithmetic, placement targeting, and the privacy constraints on health data all follow the platforms’ own public documentation, cited beneath the scenes that use them.

Coethia is a proposed system, not a shipped product. This piece describes how an engagement would be run. It is a prospectus written as a walkthrough, and it should be read as one.

The evidence on effectiveness is mixed, and shown that way. Where the research supports a claim it is cited; where effects are variable or nonsignificant the piece says so rather than quoting the best number.
