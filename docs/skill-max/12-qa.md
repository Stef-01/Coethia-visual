## The one systematic finding, measured rather than asserted

Every scene reads as floating in empty space on desktop. Quantified across all 59:

**Mean frame area actually covered by content: 59%.** So 41% of the average camera frame is empty.
The emptiest:

| scene | coverage | content | frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| `measure` | **36%** | 786×316 | 854×810 |
| `tiers` | 37% | 850×351 | 918×871 |
| `sequelae` | 40% | 745×340 | 813×771 |
| `room` | 42% | 780×368 | 848×804 |
| `weeks` | 42% | 863×402 | 931×883 |
| `cliff` | 45% | 411×734 | 845×802 |

The fullest scene reaches 78%. Nothing exceeds 100%, which is the sanity check that the measurement
is sound.

**Cause, and it is not a bug.** `fit()` expands the short side so a frame matches the stage aspect —
its own comment says *"margins, not letterbox"*, which is a deliberate choice. The desktop stage is
1.054, very nearly square. Most compositions here are wide and short: `measure` is 786×316, an aspect
of 2.49. Forcing that to 1.054 pads the height by roughly 2.5×, and the padding is the empty space.

**So this is a composition decision, not a fitter defect,** and it has exactly two honest answers:

1. **Re-compose the wide scenes to fill a near-square stage** — taller stacks, two-column layouts,
   content that uses the vertical. That is authoring work across ~20 scenes.
2. **Widen the stage** so its aspect matches what the scenes actually are. One CSS change, and it
   changes every frame, so it needs a full re-fit and re-audit behind it.

Neither is a small change and both are the owner's call. What is not defensible is leaving it
unnamed — "the scenes feel empty" is an impression; 59% mean coverage with `measure` at 36% is a fact
someone can act on.

## Method note, because the first attempt was wrong

The first coverage measurement reported 80% mean and **145% for three scenes** — impossible, since
that would mean content outside its own frame, and the audit reported zero frame overflow. The cause
was that I wrote a fresh simplified extent measurer using raw `getBBox()` instead of reusing
`measure.js`'s, dropping two load-bearing corrections: the CTM mapping into svg user space (so
anything inside a transformed group is measured in the wrong place) and skipping content clipped *by*
a clip-path.

The fix was to copy `measure.js`'s `MEASURE` **verbatim**. **Reuse the instrument; do not re-derive
it.** This was the fifth time in this pass that a finding turned out to be my own measurement, and
every one of those five would have been avoided by that rule or by a second independent path to the
same number.
