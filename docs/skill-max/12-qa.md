## The one systematic finding, measured rather than asserted

Every scene reads as floating in empty space on desktop. Quantified across all 59:

**Mean frame area actually covered by content: 59%.** So 41% of the average camera frame is empty.
The emptiest:

| scene | coverage | content | frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| `measure` | **36%** | 786×316 | 854×810 |
| `tiers` | 37% | 850×351 | 918×871 |
| `sequelae` | 40% | 745×340 | 813×771 |
| `room` | 42% | 780×368 | 848×804 |
| `weeks` | 42% | 863×402 | 931×883 |
| `cliff` | 45% | 411×734 | 845×802 |

The fullest scene reaches 78%. Nothing exceeds 100%, which is the sanity check that the measurement
is sound.

**Cause, and it is not a bug.** `fit()` expands the short side so a frame matches the stage aspect —
its own comment says *"margins, not letterbox"*, which is a deliberate choice. The desktop stage is
1.054, very nearly square. Most compositions here are wide and short: `measure` is 786×316, an aspect
of 2.49. Forcing that to 1.054 pads the height by roughly 2.5×, and the padding is the empty space.

**So this is a composition decision, not a fitter defect,** and it has exactly two honest answers:

1. **Re-compose the wide scenes to fill a near-square stage** — taller stacks, two-column layouts,
   content that uses the vertical. That is authoring work across ~20 scenes.
2. **Widen the stage** so its aspect matches what the scenes actually are. One CSS change, and it
   changes every frame, so it needs a full re-fit and re-audit behind it.

Neither is a small change and both are the owner's call. What is not defensible is leaving it
unnamed — "the scenes feel empty" is an impression; 59% mean coverage with `measure` at 36% is a fact
someone can act on.

## Method note, because the first attempt was wrong

The first coverage measurement reported 80% mean and **145% for three scenes** — impossible, since
that would mean content outside its own frame, and the audit reported zero frame overflow. The cause
was that I wrote a fresh simplified extent measurer using raw `getBBox()` instead of reusing
`measure.js`'s, dropping two load-bearing corrections: the CTM mapping into svg user space (so
anything inside a transformed group is measured in the wrong place) and skipping content clipped *by*
a clip-path.

The fix was to copy `measure.js`'s `MEASURE` **verbatim**. **Reuse the instrument; do not re-derive
it.** This was the fifth time in this pass that a finding turned out to be my own measurement, and
every one of those five would have been avoided by that rule or by a second independent path to the
same number.

## The standing rule is measurable, and it is being violated

The repo's own rule, adopted 2026-08-25 and quoted from its README:

> *no rounded rectangle may be the primary carrier of an idea. Every unit of meaning gets a drawn
> thing — a glyph, a mechanism, an instrument, a real product UI, or a figure. A box is allowed only
> as the chrome of an interface being recreated, never as a substitute for an illustration.*

This is also fault **F6** in `docs/PLAN-faster-than-the-rumour.md` — the founder's verdict on the
previous version: *"seven `roundRect`s with a tick and wrapped text… This is a slide deck drawn in
SVG."* The piece was rebuilt to remove it. `boxes.js` now checks whether it stayed removed.

### Measurable form

A rounded rect whose sibling content is **only text** is a box carrying words. One accompanied by a
path, circle, polygon, image or line is chrome around a drawn thing, which the rule permits.

Getting from that to a trustworthy number took three passes, and the intermediate numbers were all
wrong in the same direction — too high:

| pass | count | what was wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **37** | counted every text-only box, including proportional bars |
| 2 | **21** | added the distinction below; still counted controls and a cartogram |
| 3 | **7** | exempts controls and recognised chart forms |

**The distinction that mattered:** a box whose dimensions *vary across its siblings* is encoding a
quantity — a proportional bar, a gantt span, an escalating tier — and the rule does not forbid that.
`middle` is 517/143/105 wide; `weeks` is 116/232/112/232; `tiers` is 139/197/255 tall. All three are
data marks. `measure` is six identical 250×84 and `cases` three identical 240×250 — encoding nothing,
so whatever meaning they carry is carried by the words inside them. That is the slide-deck shape the
rule names. Without that distinction the check inflated 7 findings into 37.

### What it reports

```
59 scenes scanned
4 exempt as recreated interfaces: segments, subsidy, placement, privacy
data marks (permitted): middle 3, tiers 3

7 boxes carrying ONLY words, identically sized, across 2 scenes:
  measure    6    250x84  "COST PER APPOINTMENT BOOKED / $41"  …
  translate  1    273x66  "ONE EVIDENCE BASE / benefits · harms · uncertainty"
```

**`measure` is the clear violation and the worst scene in the piece by two independent measures.** Six
identical cards, each a mono eyebrow above a number, and **not one drawn thing anywhere in the
scene**. It is also the emptiest frame at **36% coverage** — content 786×316 in an 854×810 frame. A
scene that is 64% empty and made entirely of boxes is precisely what F6 described.

### One exemption I am not comfortable with

The check exempts anything with `role="button"`, on the reasoning that a control is chrome. That is
right for `k280`'s "Now all of them" and the facet chips reading "Education" — those are buttons.

But it also removes **`cases`** — three identical 240×250 cards — because they are clickable. And
`cases` is named *explicitly* in the founder's F6: *"The three case studies are three `roundRect`s
with chips."* A clickable card is still a card. So the honest count is **7 or 10 depending on whether
an interactive box gets the chrome exemption**, and I think it should not. Recorded rather than
resolved by picking the flattering number.

### What would fix `measure`

Not a nudge. Each of the four metrics needs a drawn thing: a cost per appointment is a coin or a
receipt line, a comprehension delta is a before/after pair, a false-belief movement is a needle that
moved. That is illustration work on one scene, and the repo has ~30 hand-drawn SVG illustrations
already, so it knows how. Filed rather than attempted — inventing four new glyphs for a piece with a
documented visual grammar is authoring, not QA.
