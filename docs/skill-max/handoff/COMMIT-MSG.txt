Measure the thing: ~50 legibility defects, and two instruments that can catch each other

audit.js reported ZERO text collisions across 59 scenes and had for some time. That
check compares text boxes against OTHER TEXT boxes. Three scenes then examined by hand
had, between them: a dust-ring dot inside the word MEASLES, another inside the numerals
of "R0 12-18", four annotations straddling the edge of a dark phone body, and a red
leader drawn through the "0" of "yr 4-10" so the label read as truncated. Every one is
invisible to a text-vs-text comparison, and every one is what "clunky, overlapping and
ugly" means in a screenshot.

The gap was not taste and it was not diligence in reading screenshots. It was an
instrument that measured one of two failure modes and was trusted for both.

legible.js measures the other one, in five kinds -- occluded, speckled, straddled,
low-contrast, clipped-control -- reported separately because the fixes differ.

ONE DEFECT CLASS, TEN INSTANCES: POSITIONED FROM AN ASSUMED TEXT WIDTH

Found separately, in ten places, and it is the same mistake every time:

  the video's strike-throughs   `s.length * 20.5` -- characters times a guessed advance
  countyPost's verified badge   `S.x + 52 + 128`, the name's width AT DESKTOP
  reelUI's Follow pill          placed from a measurement taken before a clamp shrank it
  reach's verified badge        `x + 94 + p.n.length * 5.3`
  mandate's circling oval       rx=112 by eye against a 169-wide block; an ellipse's
                                ends taper, so it needs (halfW/rx)^2+(halfH/ry)^2 <= 1
  placement's Ad badge          a fixed 96-unit badge behind a scaling label
  gate's PAUSED bar             a fixed 320-unit pill behind a scaling banner
  privacy's card body           a cap computed from a guessed 0.52em advance; the real
                                advance measured 0.386, so 29 chars where 39 fit
  daisy's tag cards             fields advanced by a fixed pitch while their text wrapped
  parent's fridge note          96 units of note for 114 units of text

The remedy is never a better constant. Measure it -- getComputedTextLength, measureText,
getBBox -- and re-derive anything downstream whenever that width can change, which on
this page means after applyTypeScale and after any clamp.

Two structural helpers came out of it rather than ten patches:

  data-pin   a label whose size is set by the FIXED graphic it sits inside is exempt
             from the type scale. In a facsimile the graphic is the authority and the
             type answers to it.
  haloed()   paint-order="stroke" in the LOCAL background colour, for a label that
             sits inside a field of marks. The standard cartographic answer, and one
             attribute set rather than a per-scene nudge.

Three of those ten were introduced or exposed by a fix made earlier the same day. A
measured fix that leaves an unmeasured dependency downstream has moved the bug.

THE MOBILE TRADE, STATED RATHER THAN HIDDEN

typed() paints every label at size*TK and TK reaches 3 at 375px. That is right for text
on the page and wrong for text inside a recreated device, which is a fixed width that
does not scale with it. clampPhoneText() measures and shrinks, floored at the AUTHORED
size after two other floors were tried and measured:

  no floor          audit 6 -> 15 findings, DESKTOP tiny-text in counterpost and reach
  MIN_LABEL_PX      fixed desktop; at mobile let captions stop at 7px and spill onto
                    paper at 1.2:1. Legible SIZE on the wrong background is not legible.
  authored size     fixes desktop, keeps mobile text inside its device, and costs
                    4-6 physical px for facsimile chrome

So tiny-text goes from 6 scenes to 12. The six console scenes are UNCHANGED at their
baseline 2/1/3/6/2/4; the six added are phone scenes and are this trade. None of the
three floors makes the text both legible and contained, because at 375px these devices
are too small in stage units for the copy they carry -- widen the devices, tighten the
frames, or shorten the phone copy. That is a composition decision and it is recorded as
open in docs/skill-max/handoff/RESUME-HERE.md rather than settled by whichever floor
quiets the checks.

BUILDING THE CHECK COST NINE WRONG IDEAS

Each was caught by a positive control or by rendering the scene, never by reading code:

  one threshold cannot ask two questions -- "can this hide a glyph" and "is this
    visible enough to break a word" have different answers
  do not exclude a case because the obvious test for it is bad -- strokes were skipped
    on sound reasoning about bbox tests, and the next scene examined had a leader
    through a label. Sample the geometry; demote the bbox to a prefilter
  the background is the COMPOSITE, not a layer -- picking it by area ties when text sits
    in nested shapes, and `>` then keeps the outermost. Thirty findings said white
    avatar initials were at 1.00:1 against a card BEHIND the circle they sat on
  a .42 scrim is not "too sheer to hide anything" -- discarding it measured captions
    against raw purple and the fix that report invited was to add a scrim already there
  ask the question you mean -- "covers 10-90%" is a proxy for "spans the edge", and a
    text box carries leading the glyphs do not use
  a modal is not an occlusion -- content a sheet deliberately covers is off-screen
  `closest('[role]')` finds the SVG ROOT, which carries role="group" -- a silent no-op
    that disabled the whole stroke check while appearing to work
  a line has no interior, and SVG's default fill is BLACK -- every ln() was being read
    as an opaque black rectangle of its bbox
  arriving at something is not passing through it -- connector curves terminate ON a
    card's corner by design

AND THE CHECK PASSED A BLANK PAGE

An edit deleted the `if` branch of an if/else and left the `else` orphaned. The page's
script stopped parsing; nothing rendered. legible.js walked all 59 scenes -- the steps
are static HTML -- found zero labels, and printed CLEAN with exit 0. It was reported
upstream as "87 findings -> 0". audit.js and interact.js crashed on the same page a
minute later, which is the only reason it was caught.

The positive control did not save it: that control points at a known-bad COPY, which
rendered fine. A control exercises the CHECK; it cannot tell you the SUBJECT arrived.

  syntax.js    new. Parses the inline script, 0.1s, no browser, maps errors to HTML
               lines. First gate, because a file that does not parse makes every later
               measurement meaningless.
  legible.js   counts painted labels, prints the count on every run, and exits 2 with
               NOT MEASURED rather than grading a page that did not render. Verified by
               deliberately re-breaking a copy: exit 2 broken, exit 0 good.
  shot.js      one scene, one width, 3x, so a single finding can be looked at instead of
               argued about. audit.js --shots is for a sweep; this is for a verdict, and
               it is how both remaining findings were shown to be instrument bugs. 3x
               matters: at 1x a 7px mobile label is 7 device pixels and a dot through a
               glyph is indistinguishable from antialiasing, which is the exact
               judgement it exists to support.

An instrument's most dangerous output is a pass it was not entitled to give.

AND THEN MY OWN FIXES BROKE THE OTHER CHECK

The wraps above introduced 4 text-collisions where audit.js had reported none: daisy x3,
parent, afterwords, cliff x2. Wrapping a label changes how much vertical room it needs,
and everything positioned after it has to be told -- daisy advanced its fields by a
fixed 26-unit pitch while label()'s lead for 9.4 at TK 2.6 is about 32.

All four are fixed and the fix is measured, not assumed: daisy's card heights now derive
from the wrapped line count and each advance is max(pitch, lines * leading); parent's note
widened to 132 units at mobile with the label measured to noteW - 12, so note and text
cannot drift apart; afterwords measured to pw - 20 with both lines lifted clear of the
hand; cliff reverted. audit.js reports 0 text-collisions across 59 scenes at both widths.

cliff is the cleanest lesson. Moving its tick labels down to clear the slider thumb
traded a 19-sq-px overlap for two real collisions -- and the overlap was not a defect at
all: a handle covering the tick it rests on is what every real slider does, because that
tick IS the value it reports. Reverted and left as intended.

I built legible.js for the mode audit.js could not see, then broke the mode audit.js
could. Neither instrument alone would have caught my work. That is the argument for both.
