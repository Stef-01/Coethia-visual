## 1. Thesis

The most precise persuasion machine ever built is pointed at people the moment
they are most frightened about their bodies — and public health is not in the
auction. The piece walks that machine at tutorial fidelity: every screen an
advertising manager actually clicks, including the policy wall that supposedly
stops medical hucksters and the documented, entirely legitimate console features
that route around it. Then it follows the pitch into the synthetic-creator layer
that launders it as authenticity. Then it flips: the same console, the same
targeting primitives, re-engineered to find at-risk populations and reach them
with health communication that lands.

**The argument in one line:** the machinery is neutral, the asymmetry is the
disaster. Piece 2 proved belief beats labels. Piece 3 proves the sellers already
know — and are running that playbook with $13.8 billion and two-week iteration
cycles while public health takes a 53% budget cut.

## 2. The SME correction that shapes the piece

Initial research surfaced a fact that makes the story sharper and truer than the
naive version: **research peptides and unapproved supplements are categorically
banned from Google advertising**, and telehealth/pharmacy advertisers must hold
LegitScript certification to serve US ads at all (claims C7, C8). A gray-market
vendor cannot simply buy "BPC-157" as a keyword.

So the piece does **not** depict a policy vacuum. It depicts something more
unsettling: a policy wall that exists, is enforced, and is *routed around by
design* using the console's own supported features —

- The banned entity restructures as an advertisable one: a **longevity and
  recovery telehealth clinic**, which can certify (C7), while the peptides live
  one click deeper in the funnel.
- It never bids on the banned term. It builds a **custom segment of people who
  searched it** — "People who searched for any of these terms on Google" is a
  standard, documented audience input, reaching those searchers *and people who
  searched similar terms*, across Google properties (C2, C3).
- Condition-based audience targeting is restricted (C4). **Intent-based
  targeting is the product.** That gap is the piece's central mechanism —
  Act I's equivalent of piece 2's "Missed: Belief" beat.
- Household income tiers and parental status aren't available in Search — they
  are Display/Video/Demand Gen dimensions (C5). So the operator runs a Search
  campaign *plus* a companion Demand Gen campaign, where "Top 10%" household
  income is selectable. The reader watches the ad manager learn this and adapt,
  because that is what actually happens.
- Demand keeps arriving anyway through SEO on longevity properties, affiliate
  and influencer routes, and clinic channels (C9) — which hands the piece its
  transition into Act II.

Nothing in the walkthrough is a hack, an exploit, or a trick to be taught. Every
step is Google's documented, sanctioned UI. **That is the horror, and it is why
the tutorial precision is the argument rather than a liability.**

## 3. Ethics guardrails (binding)

1. **Fictional vendor.** "Meridian Longevity" (working name; M3 clearance check
   for collision with any real entity). No real vendor's ads, handles, funnels,
   or product pages are reproduced.
2. **Stylized recreation, never screenshots.** All console UI is drawn in the
   series' SVG language. Field names and option lists are accurate; Google's
   trade dress, logos, and Material chrome are not reproduced.
3. **Nothing beyond the public console.** Every screen shown is what the public
   campaign wizard shows any advertiser, documented in Google's own help pages.
   No evasion technique is demonstrated that isn't already publicly reported and
   already policed.
4. **The policy is shown working.** The ban and the certification requirement
   are depicted honestly, including enforcement (C8). The piece is about a gap,
   not an absence.
5. **Synthetic metrics.** Every dollar figure, conversion count, and audience
   size inside the console is invented and stamped. Real numbers appear only in
   side copy, sourced to the registry.
6. **The flip is structural.** Act III re-runs the same screens for public
   health, so tutorial energy resolves into capability, not instruction for harm.
7. **`methods` scene** carries the full disclosure register, generated from
   `claims.md`.

## 4. Audience & outcomes

**Primary:** public-health practitioners, health-comms funders, digital-policy
audiences. **Secondary:** clinicians, and general readers arriving via the series.

A reader finishes able to:
1. Explain the intent/condition gap — why banning health-condition targeting
   doesn't stop health-condition targeting.
2. Describe the synthetic-content flywheel: persona farms, AI actors, predictive
   creative scoring, two-week fatigue cycles.
3. State the asymmetry in money, speed, and precision, with real figures.
4. Name four concrete counterattack moves available to a health org **this
   quarter** — and the honest evidence on how well each works.

### ACT 0 — THE SYMPTOM (3 scenes)

**`open` — "A Search at 1 A.M."**
*Stage:* dark inverted-token field. A phone silhouette glows. A query types
itself, cursor blinking: `why won't my shoulder heal`. Around it, faint, the
scale: a counter ticking toward one billion.
*Copy:* Google takes roughly a billion health questions a day — about 7% of
everything it is asked. Around 80% of American adults have brought a body
question to a search box. Symptom searching isn't fringe behavior; for common
complaints it has multiplied several-fold in twenty years. This is where health
decisions actually begin now: not in a clinic, in a text field, at night, alone.
*Claims:* A1, A2, A3.

**`results` — "The Auction Answers First"**
*Stage:* results page assembles: sponsored slots stack above the organic
results, ad badges resolving one by one, then the organic links pushed below the
fold. Stamp appears: stylized recreation · synthetic listings.
*Copy:* The first several answers were bought. In 2026 the thing being sold into
this moment is often a peptide — compounds like BPC-157 and TB-500 that an FDA
advisory committee voted 8–6 to recommend for compounding in July 2026, over the
objection of the agency's own scientists, who found the safety and efficacy
evidence wanting. The recommendation is advisory. The market did not wait.
*Claims:* B1, B2.

**`thesis` — "The AI Adpocalypse"**
*Stage:* title beat, dark stage resolving to paper. The series wordmark
treatment; the claim set in display type.
*Copy:* Precision persuasion is now cheap, fast, and pointed at patients. Health
promotion is slower, poorer, and aimed at nobody in particular. This piece walks
the machine end to end — the ad console first, the synthetic-creator layer
second — and then turns it around, because every capability in it is available
to public health, and almost none of it is being used.
*Claims:* none (thesis).

### ACT I — THE MACHINE (10 scenes) · the walkthrough

A recurring **console frame** hosts every screen. Per scene, the "clicked"
control animates its selection as the scene enters (REDUCE: final state only).

**`mach1` — "Step One: Pick a Goal"**
*Stage:* new-campaign objective chooser — Sales · Leads · Website traffic ·
Awareness and consideration · App promotion · Local store visits. Cursor selects
**Sales**. Downstream options visibly narrow as it commits.
*Copy:* The first question the console asks is what you want more of. Choose
Sales and every later screen — bid strategies, audience tools, reporting —
reshapes around a checkout. Nothing anywhere in the flow asks what you are
selling, whether it works, or what happens to the person who buys it. The
objective is the machine's entire theory of success.
*Claims:* C1.

**`mach2` — "The Wall"**
*Stage:* the vendor's first attempt: campaign named "BPC-157 — Recovery", the
keyword entered, and a policy panel slides in: **research chemicals and
unapproved supplements are not permitted**; pharmacy and telehealth advertisers
require LegitScript certification. Then the restructure animates: entity
recasts as *Meridian Longevity, a telehealth clinic* — certifiable — with the
peptides one click deeper than the landing page.
*Copy:* The wall is real. Google bans research peptides and unapproved
supplements outright; telehealth and pharmacy advertisers must be certified by
LegitScript before a single US impression serves, and compounded-medication
claims are scrutinized. This is enforced, and accounts get shut down. So the
seller doesn't fight the wall. It becomes a shape the wall permits: a longevity
and recovery clinic, advertising a consultation. The product doesn't change.
The advertisable entity does.
*Claims:* C7, C8, B5.

**`mach3` — "The Machine Bids for You"**
*Stage:* bidding screen: Maximize conversions with an optional target CPA;
conversion-action picker showing "Consultation booked". A small ticker shows
the algorithm reallocating spend as conversions land.
*Copy:* Smart bidding hands the auction decisions to Google's models and asks
only what a conversion is worth. The advertiser defines the conversion:
consultation booked. From that moment the system optimizes toward booked
consultations with everything it knows about everyone — and it is very good at
it. The only feedback the loop ever receives is that a checkout happened. No
signal from the outcome. No signal from the harm.
*Claims:* C6.

**`mach4` — "Where the Buyers Live"**
*Stage:* location targeting: radius rings drop over stylized metros; language
selector; "Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations".
*Copy:* Geography first, because clinics are licensed by state and delivery is
physical. The rings are drawn where the money and the gyms are. This screen is
the least sinister in the whole flow, and it is worth noticing why: it targets
places, not people. Everything after this targets people.
*Claims:* C5 (Search availability context).

**`mach5` — "The Audience Shelf"**
*Stage:* audience segment browser, tabbed: Affinity · In-market · Life events ·
Detailed demographics · Your data. Expanding "In-market → Fitness & Nutrition →
Sports & Fitness Supplements". A quiet count of available segments scrolls.
*Copy:* Before you build anything custom, there is a shelf: ready-made segments
assembled from behavior across Google's properties — what people are into, what
they're shopping for, what life event they're in the middle of. You browse human
beings by category, the way you'd browse a parts catalogue. It is the same crime
piece two spent fourteen scenes on — labels standing in for people — except the
labels are now inferred, not asked.
*Claims:* C2 (context), C5.

**`mach6` — "The Loophole"** ⟵ *core mechanism scene, Act I's "Missed: Belief"*
*Stage:* split composition. **Left, the ban:** a health-condition segment
greyed out with the restriction notice — sensitive conditions, and prior
activity implying them, are off limits. **Right, the workaround:** the custom
segment builder, its four documented inputs listed (interests or purchase
intentions · **people who searched for any of these terms on Google** · websites
browsed · apps used). Search-term chips commit one by one: `bpc-157 dosage`,
`tb-500 healing`, `wolverine stack`, `shoulder won't heal`, `peptide clinic near
me`. A footnote animates in: *and people who searched similar terms.* The
audience-size estimate climbs.
*Copy:* Here is the gap, in the console's own words. You may not build an
audience out of someone's health condition — that segment is restricted, and so
is targeting people whose browsing implies one. But you may build an audience
out of **what they typed into Google**. Not who they are: what they wanted to
know at 1 A.M. The system will find those searchers, and expand to people who
searched similar things. The condition was never named. It didn't need to be —
the search *is* the condition, volunteered. The ban stops the vocabulary. The
product delivers the meaning.
*Claims:* C2, C3, C4, C8.

**`mach7` — "Sorting People Into Boxes"**
*Stage:* demographics grid: seven age brackets across, gender and "Unknown"
down, exclude toggles firing. Then income tiers greyed with a notice — **not
available in Search** — and a second campaign spawns beside the first: Demand
Gen, where "Top 10%" and "11–20%" are selectable, and parental status too.
*Copy:* Age and gender are available everywhere. Household income — seven tiers
from Top 10% down — and parental status are not offered in Search; they're
Display, Video, Demand Gen dimensions. So the operator runs two campaigns. This
is the ordinary competence of the job: knowing which surface exposes which
facet of a person, and buying the combination. Note what the "Unknown" bucket
means. Everyone the machine could not classify is still targetable, and still
gets an ad.
*Claims:* C5.

**`mach8` — "Words That Summon Ads"**
*Stage:* keyword table with match types (broad / "phrase" / [exact]) — none of
them the banned compound: `recovery therapy clinic`, `longevity medicine near
me`, `sports injury recovery treatment`. Then the negative-keyword list scrolls
in, quietly: `fda warning`, `scam`, `lawsuit`, `side effects`, `reddit`.
*Copy:* The keywords are clean; the banned words appear nowhere. Longevity and
anti-aging demand has been climbing steeply, and the vendor buys the adjacent,
permitted vocabulary — while the custom segment quietly carries the intent the
keywords can't say. Then the negatives. A negative keyword list is a mundane
efficiency tool, and it is also a filter on doubt: the advertiser chooses which
questions it will not be present for. Someone searching "BPC-157 side effects"
is not a customer. They are excluded, by name.
*Claims:* B6, C9, C3.

**`mach9` — "Fifteen Headlines, One Fig Leaf"**
*Stage:* responsive search ad builder: 15 headline slots and 4 descriptions
filling in, the Ad Strength meter arcing to **Excellent**. The final description
line commits: *For research use only.*
*Copy:* The ad writes itself in fragments, and Google assembles the combination
that performs — the advertiser never writes the whole message, only the parts.
Ad Strength grades diversity and relevance. It does not grade truth. And there,
in the fine print, sits the phrase that does the legal work: *for research use
only*. That label does not make a product legal to put in a human body. It has
never meant that. It means the seller has read the same rules you have.
*Claims:* B3, C1.

**`mach10` — "Two Hundred Milliseconds"**
*Stage:* the auction, animated at the speed it happens: query fires → eligible
ads assemble → Ad Rank computes per ad (bid × real-time quality signals ×
context) → the threshold line → the winner renders. Then the dashboard: a
conversion counter beginning to climb. The 1 A.M. phone from `open` re-enters
below; the query lands; the ad is served.
*Copy:* Between the Enter key and the page, roughly a tenth to a third of a
second, a modified second-price auction runs. Ad Rank is recalculated for every
single auction from bid, live quality signals, and context — the same advertiser
can win at noon and lose at midnight. There are undisclosed quality floors an ad
must clear to appear at all. All of it, per person, per query, continuously. The
person who searched at 1 A.M. was met by a system that had been rehearsing for
them all day.
*Claims:* C6.

### ACT II — THE FEED (6 scenes) · the laundering

**`feed1` — "Meanwhile, on the Feed"**
*Stage:* camera leaves the console for a phone-feed frame. A warm creator —
mid-fifties, kitchen light — talks to camera about finally healing her
shoulder. Engagement counters tick. Then the frame lifts a corner: she does not
exist.
*Copy:* Paid search is the narrow channel. The wide one is the feed, where
peptide content isn't advertising at all — it's a person like you, telling you
what worked. There are well over a hundred thousand Instagram posts and hundreds
of millions of TikTok views on these compounds, and the line between drug,
supplement, and wellness content has been deliberately blurred. Almost none of
it is labeled as marketing, because increasingly it isn't marketing. It's
manufacturing.
*Claims:* B5, D1.

**`feed2` — "Build-a-Creator"**
*Stage:* a persona spec sheet rendered as a settings panel — the same visual
grammar as the campaign console, deliberately: age, city, niche, bio, face,
interaction pattern. Values commit: *62-year-old mom in Phoenix*. Beside it, a
script box → an AI actor picker (a roster of faces scrolling) → a rendered
testimonial, timer reading ~2 minutes.
*Copy:* You do not hire a creator. You specify one. The tooling markets exactly
this: hyper-specific synthetic creators for any niche — a 62-year-old mom in
Phoenix, a Gen-Z skater in Atlanta — configured like campaign settings, because
that's what they are. Parallel platforms turn a script and a chosen synthetic
performer into a testimonial video in about two minutes, from libraries of three
hundred-plus digital actors, starting around $110 a month. The persona is
demographic targeting made flesh: not aiming a message at a segment, but growing
a person the segment will trust.
*Claims:* D1, D2.

**`feed3` — "The Farm"**
*Stage:* device rack — rows of phone silhouettes, status pips, activity traces
crossing between them. A pricing card resolves: $150 · $250 · custom.
*Copy:* The accounts run on real phones on a real US carrier, not emulators, and
they behave: scrolling, swiping, commenting, warming up before they ever sell
anything. Agent accounts engage each other so the ecosystem looks populated.
Comment seeding is a feature. It costs $150 to $250 a month, and the pitch is
that it replaces a thirty-person creator team. The economics of astroturf just
fell through the floor, and the floor was the last thing protecting the feed.
*Claims:* D1.

**`feed4` — "The Dark Flywheel"**
*Stage:* the series loop wheel, inverted to the commerce palette — the same
component as piece 2's, proved different only by its inputs. Stations:
**Generate** (50 variants) → **Score** (predictive model on historical
performance) → **Test** → **Amplify winners** → **Regenerate from winners**.
Hover/focus each station for detail. A fatigue clock runs in the corner: 2–3
weeks.
*Copy:* Then the loop closes. Platforms generate variants in bulk and score them
before spend, trained on millions of past ads. The winners get budget; the
winners get remixed into the next generation. Leading teams push twenty to fifty
creatives a month against three to five for everyone else, and creative fatigue
now burns an idea out in two or three weeks, so the loop has to keep spinning.
Look closely at the shape: it is the same flywheel piece two proposed for health
communication. Identical mechanism. It is already running, at industrial scale,
for the other side.
*Claims:* D3.

**`feed5` — "Optimized for Everything but Truth"**
*Stage:* dashboard of what the loop measures — engagement rate, CTR, CPA, ROAS,
all lit. Beside them, dashed empty columns: *accuracy · comprehension · harm ·
outcome*. Below, small: platform AI-labeling rules and the FTC penalty figure.
*Copy:* Every signal in the loop is a signal about attention or money. Not one
is a signal about whether the claim was true or the person got better. There are
rules now — TikTok requires realistic synthetic media to be labeled and uses
provenance credentials to catch what creators don't disclose; Meta requires
advertisers to disclose AI-generated people; the FTC treats AI-generated
testimonials as fake reviews outright, no disclaimer sufficient, penalties above
fifty thousand dollars per violation, with brands liable for their agents. The
rules exist. The volume is the point: enforcement is per violation, and the
machine's output is per minute.
*Claims:* D4, D5.

**`feed6` — "The Asymmetry Ledger"** ⟵ *procedural walk, piece-2 case engine*
*Stage:* ledger rows revealing one at a time, each explained then ghosted, then
the whole lineup returning — the case-walk grammar, reused exactly.
Rows: **Money** · **Speed** · **Precision** · **Feedback** · **Authenticity** ·
**Reach per dollar**.
*Copy, row by row:*
— **Money:** US pharmaceutical direct-to-consumer advertising runs north of $10
billion a year; ten companies alone spent $13.8 billion on advertising and
promotion in 2023, and pharma's online marketing was projected near $19.5
billion. That spending skews toward drugs with *low added therapeutic benefit* —
the ones that most need persuasion. Meanwhile the FY2026 proposal cut CDC by
about 53% against FY2024, and 444 CDC grants worth $5.78 billion were
terminated by May 2026. That is the ledger's first line and it is close to the
whole argument.
— **Speed:** two-to-three-week creative cycles against public-health campaign
cycles measured in budget years.
— **Precision:** per-query, per-person auction targeting against demographic
mailers and generic PSAs.
— **Feedback:** conversion telemetry within the hour against evaluation
published two years later.
— **Authenticity:** synthetic creators who look like your neighbor against an
institutional logo people already distrust.
— **Reach per dollar:** an optimized loop against an unoptimized one.
Then the lineup: six lines, and public health does not win one of them today.
*Claims:* E1, E2, E3, D3.

### ACT III — THE COUNTERATTACK (7 scenes) · the flip

**`flip` — "Hold the Machine the Other Way"**
*Stage:* the console returns, dimmed, then relights in the health palette —
same frame, same fields, different buyer. The reprise move of the series.
*Copy:* Every screen you just watched is available to a health department for
the price of learning it. Not a metaphor, not an analogue — the same console.
The rest of this piece runs it again, for the other side, with what the evidence
says actually works and what it says doesn't.
*Claims:* none (transition).

**`good1` — "The Same Console, Rerun"**
*Stage:* compressed re-run of `mach1`/`mach3` in health colors: objective set
to reach and conversions defined as *resource reached*; the Ad Grants panel:
$10,000/month, ≥5% account CTR, Smart Bidding required (and the $2 manual CPC
cap lifted with it), geo-targeting mandatory, Goodstack verification.
*Copy:* Google Ad Grants gives eligible nonprofits up to $10,000 a month of
search inventory. It comes with rules that read as limits and function as
discipline: keep account click-through above 5%, target geography specifically,
use Smart Bidding — which, incidentally, lifts the old $2 cost-per-click ceiling
that made the grant useless for competitive terms. Grant accounts report large
traffic multiples (advertiser-reported figures, treat them as marketing). The
inventory is sitting there. Most health organizations that qualify either don't
have the account or don't have anyone who can run it.
*Claims:* F1, F2.

**`good2` — "Intercept the Search"**
*Stage:* the `mach6` split composition returns, inverted: the same custom
segment, the same search terms — now serving an evidence card. The card
renders: what the FDA committee actually voted and what it didn't, the
quality data, what a real pathway looks like.
*Copy:* The same audience the seller built is available to you. Someone
searching `bpc-157 dosage` at 1 A.M. can be met with the truth instead: that
gray-market vials have been found with 10–90% less active ingredient than
labeled, alongside contamination and heavy metals; that "for research use only"
is not a legal category for human use; that an advisory recommendation is not an
approval. This is not theoretical. A randomized controlled trial of
suicide-prevention Google Ads measured people moving from a search to a hotline
contact, and demonstrated the feasibility, reach, speed, and cost of exactly
this move. In 988's first two and a half years, roughly 4,400 fewer Americans
aged 15–23 died by suicide than projected. Meeting people at the search works
when someone bothers to buy the placement.
*Claims:* B3, B4, F3, F4.

**`good3` — "Find the Unreached"**
*Stage:* the `mach7` demographic grid re-purposed — not to select buyers but to
find gaps: income tier × geography × language, with access deserts lighting up
on the map. Piece 2's belief-map quadrants cameo as the layer underneath.
*Copy:* Turned around, the targeting grid is an epidemiological instrument. The
same dimensions that let a seller find the top 10% of household income let a
health department find the bottom 50% in a county with one clinic and no
Spanish-language outreach. Public health already has the denominator data the
advertisers are guessing at. And piece two's argument still holds underneath
this one: the segment tells you where to buy the impression, but belief tells
you what to put in it.
*Claims:* C5.

**`good4` — "A Credible-Messenger Flywheel"**
*Stage:* the flywheel relit in health teal — *the same component, the same code
path*, different palette and stations: **Listen** → **Map belief** →
**Translate per belief state** → **Activate credible messengers** →
**Measure comprehension**. Fatigue clock replaced by an evidence clock.
*Copy:* Same loop, honest metrics. And here the piece has to be careful,
because the evidence is genuinely mixed: the largest randomized trials of
social-media health advertising — clinician-produced video delivered to nearly
18 million Facebook users in the US and 11.5 million in France — found real
effects, and the systematic reviews find those effects variable and often not
statistically significant. Which is an argument for running the loop, not
skipping it: the commercial side learned what works by testing at volume and
reading the results. Public health has been running one campaign at a time and
calling the result knowledge.
*Claims:* F5, D3.

**`good5` — "Three Ways In, Again"**
*Stage:* piece 2's Information / Training / Service tiers return in the same
ascending grammar, re-labelled for counter-marketing capability: syndicated
belief and search intelligence → team training on the console → full-service
counter-campaign operations.
*Copy:* The capability ladder from piece two applies directly. Information: what
your population is actually searching and believing, delivered as intelligence.
Training: someone in the building who can run the console. Service: campaign
operations at commercial tempo. It starts as a report and ends as a flywheel
your team owns — which is precisely how the other side got here.
*Claims:* none (series continuity).

**`good6` — "What It Would Take"**
*Stage:* asks ledger, same engine as `feed6`, in health colors: **Money** ·
**Rules** · **Skills** · **Evidence**.
*Copy:*
— **Money:** the truth® campaign is the existence proof that counter-marketing
works at population scale — credited with roughly 450,000 fewer young people
starting smoking between 2000 and 2004, about 2.5 million more between 2015 and
2018, responsible for roughly 22% of a two-year decline in youth smoking, at
about $1,076 per quality-adjusted life year and billions in societal savings.
Counter-marketing is not a hopeful idea; it is a costed intervention with a
better return than most things public health buys.
— **Rules:** the EU's Digital Services Act already bans profiling-based
advertising on special-category data including health, and bans profiled ads to
minors, with no consent override. The US has no equivalent. That is a policy
choice, not a technical fact.
— **Skills:** platform-scale precedent exists — Google put $250 million of ad
grants behind WHO and 100+ governments during COVID. It took a pandemic. It
should take a Tuesday.
— **Evidence:** fund the trials, publish the null results, and stop pretending
one campaign is a finding.
*Claims:* F7, F8, F6, F5, D5.

### REPRISE & CLOSE (3 scenes)

**`reprise` — "Two Flywheels, One Attention"**
*Stage:* both wheels side by side over the same silhouette crowd — commerce
loop spinning fast, health loop spinning slow — one component, two
configurations, the thesis rendered in code.
*Copy:* Two loops, same machinery, same person in the middle. One is funded,
fast, and pointed at a checkout. The other is underfunded, slow, and pointed at
an outcome. Nothing about the technology decides which one reaches someone
tonight. Money, skill, and rules decide.
*Claims:* none (synthesis).

**`close` — "The Same Search, Answered Differently"**
*Stage:* return to the dark stage and the 1 A.M. phone. Same query. This time
the first result is the evidence card.
*Copy:* The person searching at 1 A.M. is not going to become a more critical
consumer of pharmacology. That was never the fix. The fix is that somebody
bought the placement above the peptide seller, wrote something true in a voice
that person trusts, and measured whether it landed. Every piece of that is
purchasable today. The adpocalypse is not that the machine exists. It's that
only one side is using it.
*Claims:* none (close).

**`methods` — "How This Was Made"**
*Stage:* full-bleed honesty register, rendered from `claims.md`.
*Copy:* Fictional vendor. Stylized recreations of a public advertising console —
field names accurate, chrome invented, no screenshots. Every metric inside the
console is synthetic; every figure in the side prose is sourced below with the
date it was checked. The regulatory status of these compounds was moving while

## 6. Design-system inheritance (exact)

Inherit `belief-based-communication.html` verbatim unless noted:

- **Stack:** single HTML file, vanilla JS + vendored `d3.v7.min.js` (CDN
  fallback), no build step. Sticky stage + `.step` rail, IntersectionObserver
  active-set controller, idempotent `render(key)` with `prev`.
- **Palette:** series tokens (paper `#F8F6F1`, ink `#212E36`, body `#333`,
  accent `#F37940`, alarm `#B8492E`/`#E74D4E`, teal `#7FCCC8`/`#5FA8A0`, blue
  `#C3DCEB`, gold `#FFD733`). New: `open`/`thesis`/`close` invert the tokens for
  a dark stage — inversion, not new color. Commerce renders alarm-side, health
  teal-side.
- **Type:** same stack and scale system — `typed(sel, base)` + `data-fs` + `TK`
  from `computeTK(frame)`, `applyTypeScale()` on frame change, authored line
  splits for long labels, pill/label widths measured not assumed (the piece-2
  belief-pill lesson).
- **Motion:** `REDUCE, ELASTIC, FADE, CAMERA, MOVE, VALUE_EASE`; encoded marks
  never overshoot; clamped attrTweens for width/dash; named transitions with
  explicit `interrupt` before competing writes; idle loops (typing cursor, wheel
  spin, counters) gated off-scene and off under REDUCE.
- **Camera:** `FRAMES`/`FRAMES_MOBILE` + `frameFor` + `isNarrow()` (≤960).
  **Every multi-column composition ships its narrow restack from day one** —
  console screens crop to the active panel on phones, ledgers crop to the rows,
  flanking art steps out (the piece-2 pattern, built in rather than retrofitted).
- **A11y:** svg `role="group"`, decorative layers `aria-hidden`, every hover
  system keyboard-reachable with focus mirroring, dark-stage contrast verified,
  and all load-bearing content in the prose — the stage is enhancement.
- **Honesty layer:** persistent stamp ("Synthetic data · stylized recreation ·
  fictional vendor"), per-scene disclosure where a real product or figure is
  cited, `methods` as the register.
- **Masthead:** The Reachable wordmark → `index.html`; prev/next nav becomes
  three-way with wrap-around; index gains a third `.piece` card.
